First we need to set up some terms and concepts that we will need to know here

Prop Size   

Example(5 inch)

This is diameter

Five is common on freestyle

Bigger prop = more power but less efficient and less agile, smaller prop = less power but more efficient and more agile

Blade Count (bi-blade, tri-blade)

tri-blade = 3 blades  

bi-blade = 2 blades

Bi-blade is more efficient, more flight time, less power, more agile, less grip on the air, less stable

Biblade is more efficient, more flight time, less power, more agile, less grip on the air, less stable

Tri-blade is less efficient, less flight time, more power, less agile, more grip on the air, more stable

Pitch (Very important)

Exemple(4.6 inch, 5.1 inch)

Pitch is how agressive the prop is, higher pitch = more aggressive, lower pitch = less aggressive

Low pitch(3.0) is smooth efficient and good for beginners, and has less thrust than high pitch(4.6+) that is more aggressive and better for racing  but not as efficient and harder to control for beginners

The rule is: freestyle medium pitch(4.5-4.3)

High pitch(4.6+) is for racing.

Material

Polycarbonate (PC) | This is the most common material for props, it's cheap, durable, and has good performance. It's a good choice for beginners and general use.

Carbon Fiber (CF) | This is a more expensive material, it's lighter and stiffer than polycarbonate, which can improve performance and efficiency. However, it's also more brittle and can break more easily, especially in crashes. It's a good choice for advanced pilots and racing.
